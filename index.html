A Better way to think about Quantum Computers

Let's look past the pop-science buzzwords and build a clear, honest mental model together.

The Myth

There’s this common misconception about Quantum Computers:

📌
“Because qubits, unlike bits, can be both 0 and 1 at once, qubits can go through a quantum circuit and get the result of all combination of bits at once — thus quantum computers are exponentially faster than classical bits.”
⤷ You've probably heard this in tech news or pop-science articles!
The Reality Check
⚠️ This is NOT true.

And it leads to a misalignment between expectations between the public and “quantum people” (i.e. engineers, researchers and quantum developers).

Public Expectation:

“A magic super-processor that checks every password and searches all possibilities in parallel!”

VS
Quantum Reality:

“A delicate, precise linear algebra machine where you can only measure ONE classical answer.”

💡

But is there a better, more accurate mental model of quantum computing?

Wild Physics

Quantum Mechanics is super mysterious and interesting.

🌀

👻

🌌

🐈

Schrödinger's cat: alive and dead at the same time until opened.

✨ All sorts of weird and wild physics!
The Natural Assumption

You would think, by extension, quantum computing must also be mysterious.

And hence, a simple mental model would be impossible.

The Counter-Argument

However, that argument doesn’t hold weight.

Why? Because classical, everyday computers are also based on some very complex technology, but all they are trying to do is make a specific mathematical object be realised in the real world.

Though the Turing Machine is very simple, even the most sophisticated computers in the world are, at the end of the day, physical realisations of Turing machines. If you understand Turing machines, you still have a LOT to learn about computers, but you have got the mental model right.

⭐ The obvious question now is: What is the mathematical blueprint of the quantum computer? Can it also give an intuitive understanding of why you can’t make a true quantum computer out of classical parts?
A Classical Problem

Let’s discuss a classical problem first. It is called the #SAT problem.

You create a (classical!) logic circuit with $n$ switches and 1 output, and you want to know the total number of times that output is 1, across all possible combinations of ON and OFF switches.

The Implicit Object

When you define this problem, a giant, invisible object is defined implicitly:

📜 The Full Truth Table.

If you could simply write down all $2^n$ possible input combinations on a sheet of paper and count the matches, the problem would be solved!

The Classical Catch

Here’s the catch: for just 300 switches, $2^{300}$ is larger than all the atoms in the entire observable universe!

👻 In a classical computer, that giant table is a GHOST

It does NOT physically exist inside your laptop. Your CPU only holds one single row ($n$ bits) in its registers at any microsecond.

The full table of $2^n$ rows lives purely as an abstract mathematical idea in our heads.

The Quantum Reality

Now, here is the magic of quantum computing:

In a quantum computer, that exponentially huge object actually exists in the real world. When you have $n$ qubits, nature doesn’t just give you $n$ separate switches. It gives you a state vector living in a $2^n$-dimensional complex vector space.

The Secret Sauce

Compressible Product vs. The Entangled Beast

However, if there is no entanglement between qubits, the state vector is easily compressible — i.e. it doesn’t contain an exponential amount of information, and can be reduced to a product of the state vectors of the individual qubits.

🟢 WITHOUT ENTANGLEMENT (Separable State) Easily Compressible & Classical
$$(a_0|0\rangle + b_0|1\rangle) \otimes (a_1|0\rangle + b_1|1\rangle) \otimes (a_2|0\rangle + b_2|1\rangle) \otimes \dots \otimes (a_{n-1}|0\rangle + b_{n-1}|1\rangle)$$

The state factors completely into individual independent qubits. It requires only $2 \times n$ numbers to describe! Any everyday classical smartphone can effortlessly simulate this in milliseconds.

VS
🟣 WITH ENTANGLEMENT (Irreducible Superposition) The Exponential Beast
$$|\psi\rangle = \sum_{x \in \{0,1\}^n} c_x |x\rangle = c_{00\dots0}|00\dots0\rangle + c_{00\dots1}|00\dots1\rangle + \dots + c_{11\dots1}|11\dots1\rangle$$

This factorization is strictly impossible in general! You cannot split the qubits apart. You have an irreducible $2^n$-dimensional state vector physically instantiated in the laboratory, created from just $n$ physical qubits!

🦋 Live Quantum Test: 2 Qubits (Repeated 1,024 Times) See how entanglement changes the measured frequency of outcomes
1. The Separable State: $|+\rangle \otimes |+\rangle$

Two independent qubits, each prepared in a 50/50 superposition like two independent tossed coins. Because they have zero coupling, knowing the outcome of Qubit 0 tells you nothing about Qubit 1.

$$|\psi\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}(|00\rangle + |01\rangle + |10\rangle + |11\rangle)$$
2. The Entangled Bell State: $|\Phi^+\rangle$

The two qubits are linked together by a quantum gate into an entangled state. They no longer have separate individual identities: if Qubit 0 collapses to 0, Qubit 1 is guaranteed to be 0; if 1, Qubit 1 is guaranteed to be 1!

$$|\Phi^+\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$$

Physical Control

You might ask — how is this different from the exponential object just being a “ghost”?

Well, the cool part is that, unlike the classical situation, you can make qubits go through quantum gates to affect and alter this state vector! So a 300 qubit quantum computer, irreducibly entangled, would be manipulating a $2^{300}$ long state vector. Isn’t that crazy?

A small set of physical knobs manipulating a colossal mathematical world!
The Catch

Ah! But here’s the catch —

You cannot actually see and probe the state vector directly. What you can do is make a measurement, which forces the state vector to collapse into just one possibility among the $2^n$. That’s all you can probe.

The Core Strategy

How quantum algorithms actually work:

Pretty much all quantum algorithms are all about manipulating the state vector using quantum gates, such that the amplitudes in the state vector (the relative “weight” of each possibility) becomes concentrated in one or very few possibilities, revealing the answer!

But you can never view the full object directly — when you make a measurement, you simply let the stick fall flat and observe a single outcome!

Why Not Classical?

Why can’t you do this state vector business with classical components?

Like switches, transistors and stuff? We have no known classical system that allows you to use a few knobs to manipulate an exponentially larger object compared to those knobs. We only see this behaviour in (very delicate!) quantum systems.

Classical Switches

5 switches ➔ 5 physical wires.

Each knob only affects its own wire. No exponential leverage exists.

VS
Quantum Qubits

5 qubits ➔ 32-dimensional complex vector space.

Each gate operation rotates the global state across the entire $2^n$ space!

The Takeaway

A Much Better Mental Model

Instead of the “all possibilities at once” misconception, here’s a much better mental model of quantum computers:

⭐ ⭐ ⭐
“Quantum computers allow you to dial knobs to manipulate an object that is exponentially larger than the number of knobs — but you don’t see that object directly (nor would you want to — merely describing the full object could take an incredibly long time); you make a measurement to collapse that object into only one of the exponential number of possibilities; and if you’ve set up the quantum gates to manipulate qubits the way you wanted to, the measured possibility contains some or all of the information that you wanted to get out of this entire computation.”
🎉 The End

That’s a mouthful, but certainly a much more accurate picture of what’s going on with quantum computers!
